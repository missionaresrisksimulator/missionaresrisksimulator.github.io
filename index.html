01

BASE ENVIRONMENT

The mission takes place on real terrain within Jezero Crater, Mars (18.427° N, 77.437° E), modeled using high-resolution orbital images captured by the HiRISE camera (ESP_045994_1985).

02

AEROSPACE INTERFACE (UI)

The interface features a high-contrast color palette inspired by real aerospace control centers, combining deep blues, clean whites, and safety oranges. It uses easy-to-read typography and panels with simplified buttons and visual indicators, specifically designed to allow young people aged 7 to 12 to operate the station intuitively and effortlessly.

03

MISSION FLOW (UX)

The system guides players through a progressive learning curve divided into four stages: Introduction → Training → Mission → Evaluation, ensuring they master life support systems before facing resource management challenges under pressure.

M.A.R.S. / MISSION SYSTEMS

Beyond the mission.

An experience designed to explore, learn, and understand the challenges of building a habitable base on Mars.

MISSION LOCATION

Jezero Crater.

Jezero Crater is the setting for our mission. There, junior astronauts must understand how to manage the resources and conditions required to sustain a Martian base.

EDUCATIONAL PROFILE

Learning through exploration.

M.A.R.S. is primarily designed for children and young people, offering a visual, intuitive, and experimental experience. It also welcomes participants aged 16 and above.

MISSION FLOW

Four stages. One mission.

Each stage represents a new step in the preparation and survival journey of the junior commander.

01
INTRODUCTION

First Contact

Understanding the challenge and introducing the mission environment at Jezero Crater.

02
TRAINING

Preparing for Survival

Becoming familiar with life support indicators, radiation levels, energy systems, and food supplies.

03
MAIN MISSION

Decisions Under Pressure

Managing difficult scenarios and making decisions while working with limited resources.

04
EVALUATION

The Mission Outcome

Performance evaluation and the awarding of badges or medals.

HABITAT ARCHITECTURE

Inside the habitat.

Four connected areas representing the main activities within a Martian base.

01
HABITATION MODULE

Main Habitat

The central living and operations area. It includes sleeping quarters, a rest area, and environmental control consoles used to monitor oxygen levels, temperature, and life support.

02
AIRLOCK SYSTEM

Airlocks

Depressurization chambers designed to prepare spacesuits and teach the necessary sequence of procedures before stepping onto the Martian surface.

03
INFLATABLE HABITAT

Inflatable Habitat

A 16-meter expansion area designed for laboratories and storage. It can serve as a research space for life sciences and geophysics.

04
EXTERNAL MONITORING

Monitoring Station

An outdoor area for interpreting weather conditions, wind speed, and data received through communication antennas.
